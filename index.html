How to Use the Word Unscrambler

The Word Unscrambler takes a jumbled set of letters and instantly finds every valid English word that can be formed from them. Simply type or paste your scrambled letters into the input box and press Unscramble. The tool searches through a large dictionary and returns all matching words in seconds, grouped by length so you can quickly spot the longest and highest-scoring options. No installation is needed — everything runs in your browser and your letters are never sent to a server.

This tool is particularly useful for word games such as Scrabble, Words With Friends, Wordle, Boggle, and crossword puzzles where finding high-value words from a constrained set of tiles can make all the difference. It also works as a vocabulary builder: seeing which words can be formed from random letters exposes you to words you might not otherwise encounter. The unscrambler handles between two and fifteen letters, covers standard two-letter words through to uncommon but valid longer words, and updates its results in real time as you change the input.

If you are working on a specific word puzzle that requires a fixed prefix, suffix, or a blank tile (wildcard), you can use the wildcard feature — enter a question mark or asterisk to represent any letter. This is especially helpful when you know one letter must appear in a certain position and you want to limit results accordingly. The tool instantly reorders matches to show the most useful combinations for your situation.

When to Use the Word Unscrambler

  • Scrabble & Words With Friends: Find the highest-scoring word from your current rack of tiles before your turn runs out.
  • Wordle / Daily Puzzles: Rearrange the letters you've confirmed to discover what word fits the pattern.
  • Crossword clues: Unscramble an anagram clue quickly without working through every permutation manually.
  • Boggle: Spot longer words hiding inside your grid of letters before time is up.
  • Learning & teaching: Use it as a vocabulary exercise — scramble a word and let students guess before revealing the answer.
  • Anagram puzzles: Solve creative anagrams in pub quizzes, trivia nights, or magazine games.
Example 1: Letters: aelrts → Results include alerts, alters, laster, slater, staler, tarsel — six different six-letter words from the same six letters.
Example 2: Letters: listen → The unscrambler reveals silent, enlist, tinsel, inlets — a classic family of anagrams that surprises many players.

Frequently Asked Questions

What dictionary does the Word Unscrambler use?

The unscrambler checks against a standard English word list that includes words accepted in both Scrabble (TWL / SOWPODS) and everyday use. Proper nouns, abbreviations, and hyphenated words are generally excluded because they are not valid in most word games.

Does it work for two-letter words?

Yes. Two-letter words like qi, za, jo, and ax are included, making this especially valuable for Scrabble players who need to play parallel or perpendicular to existing tiles on the board.

Can I use a blank tile (wildcard)?

Yes — type a question mark (?) in place of your blank tile and the tool will find words that can be made using any letter in that position. Wildcard results are slightly broader, so you may see more options than usual.

Is the Word Unscrambler free to use?

Completely free. There is no sign-up, no usage limit, and no data is transmitted to any server. All processing happens locally in your browser.

How many letters can I unscramble at once?

The tool handles inputs of up to fifteen letters. For very long inputs the number of possible combinations grows exponentially, but the search algorithm is optimised to return results quickly even at that length.
